Abstract (1,638 characters -- verified under arXiv's 1,920-character hard limit):
Prompts for Large Language Models (LLMs) often grow long, costly, and fragile as instructions, few-shot examples, and safety rules accumulate by trial and error. Automatic prompt optimizers such as DSPy MIPROv2 automate prompt writing, but they typically produce opaque 1,100+ token prompts without proving which pieces actually improve accuracy, whether rare safety rules survive, or whether small formatting changes break performance. We present PromptPrism 2.1, an open-source statistical auditing framework that tests prompt components in balanced experiments and certifies prompt stability. Compared directly against DSPy MIPROv2 on gemini-2.5-flash across three benchmarks (Banking77, SQuAD 2.0, and GSM8K; N=240 held-out items), PromptPrism delivers three practical benefits: (1) safe bloat removal -- auditing compiled DSPy prompts reveals that DSPy's generated instructions are statistically dead weight on Banking77 and SQuAD 2.0, cutting prompt length by 39%-57% with matched accuracy and passing stability bounds; (2) rare guardrail protection -- a coverage-aware pruning gate cuts accidental deletion of rare safety rules from 18%-95% down to 0% in calibration and catches 85% of out-of-scope Banking77 queries where DSPy scores 0%; and (3) compact, stable standalone prompts -- using structured multi-turn XML framing without DSPy or an expensive proposer model, PromptPrism matches or edges DSPy on Banking77 (96.3% vs. 95.8%, -39% tokens) and GSM8K (95.8% vs. 94.6%, -67% tokens), passes strict perturbation stability across all three tasks, and shows why structural scaffolds must stay fixed when pruning redundant pieces.
